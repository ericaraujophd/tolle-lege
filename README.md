# Tolle Lege

*"Take up and read."* Augustine, *Confessions* VIII.12

Pocket booklets of public-domain classics, **3.5 × 5 in**, ready to print at home,
fold, sew through the fold and trim. Each print run makes two copies.

**Just want to print one?** Go to the website:
**https://ericaraujo.com/tolle-lege/**. Every book has its two print files
(interior and cover) and the step-by-step guide. Nothing to install.

This README is for building the files yourself or adding a new title.

The rule of thumb: **anything shared lives in `tools/`, anything specific to one
book lives in its `titles/` folder, and `docs/` is generated.** Fix the cutting
marks once in `tools/impose.py` and every title gets the fix on the next build.

## Building

Requires Python 3.11 or newer.

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
python3 build.py                # every title + the website, into docs/
python3 build.py --only 01-aquinas-on-curiosity   # one title, site untouched
python3 build.py --extract      # also regenerate content.typ from the sources
```

For each available title the build makes, in `docs/books/<title>/`:

| File | What it is | Print on |
|---|---|---|
| `<title>-interior.pdf` | the inside pages on letter sheets, 2 copies per sheet | 24 lb text paper, double-sided, flip on long edge |
| `<title>-cover.pdf` | the cover, 2 copies on one sheet | 65 lb cardstock, double-sided |
| `<title>-reading.pdf` | one page per booklet page, in reading order | nothing: for reading and proofreading |
| `cover.png` | the front cover, for the website | |

The interior is always padded to a multiple of 4 pages with ruled notes pages
(a folded sheet carries 4 pages); the last notes page carries the colophon.

### Correcting for your printer

Many printers put the back side a millimetre or two off. The files on the
website are uncorrected so they work everywhere. To make files for *your*
printer:

1. Print `docs/tools/alignment-test.pdf` double-sided (flip on long edge, 100%).
2. Hold it to a window with the front facing you. Read where the back crosshair
   sits compared with the front one, in mm (ticks are 1 mm): right is positive,
   up is positive.
3. `python3 build.py --back-offset 2 1` (with your numbers). The corrected files
   go into `build/` (ignored by git), never into `docs/`.

## Printing marks

Only on the front of each sheet, all in the waste that gets cut off:

- **Dashed line** across the middle, "cut" at each end: cut here first.
- **Corner crosses** (light grey) at every page corner: the trim lines. Each line
  runs past the corner and 0.35 in along the page edge, so trimming one edge
  never removes the mark for another: trim the four edges in any order. Cut *on*
  the line.
- **Fold marks**: short stems at the top and bottom centre.
- **Sheet label** in the left margin ("No. 01 interior sheet 3 of 6, front") and
  the cutting order in the right margin.

## Making the booklet

The illustrated version is on the website. In short:

1. **Cut** every sheet on the dashed line. Top halves and bottom halves go in two
   piles; each pile is one book.
2. **Check the order** with the sheet labels: sheet 1 outside.
3. **Fold** each half sheet through the fold marks, "front" outside.
4. **Nest** the last sheet inside the one before, out to sheet 1, then into the
   (scored and folded) cover. Square the spine and head.
5. **Sew** down the fold, cover side up: denim needle 100/16, top-stitch thread,
   longest straight stitch, no backstitch; tie the tails inside at the centre
   spread. (Or a three-hole pamphlet stitch by hand, or two staples.)
6. **Trim** on the corner crosses, any order.
7. **Round** the two fore-edge corners with a 1/4 in (R6) corner rounder.

Per 25 copies: 13 print runs.

## Adding a title

1. Copy `titles/01-aquinas-on-curiosity/` to `titles/NN-author-on-subject/`.
2. Edit `title.toml` (set `slug` to the folder name).
3. Put the source text in `source/` and write `extract.py` to turn it into
   `content.typ` (or write `content.typ` by hand for a short text). The template
   offers `#chapter(label, name)`, `#section(num, title: ...)`, and the Summa's
   `#question` / `#article`; pass the ones you use in the `scope` in `book.typ`.
4. Rewrite `intro.typ` and `reflection.typ`.
5. `python3 build.py`, check the reading copy, print a test, commit.

**Copyright check before adding a title:** the text *and* the translation must
be in the public domain in the United States. Works first published in 1930 or
earlier are; later translations usually are not.

## The notebooks

Pocket notebooks at the same 3.5 × 5 in size, with a silhouette cover. Only the
cover is designed here; for the inside, buy letter-size dotted, lined or blank
paper, cut it in half and fold (lined paper: lines running across the sheet's
short side, so they stay horizontal on the page). The front is deliberately
bare: the small silhouette and the series name. The inside back has three
boxes, Dotted, Lined, Blank: tick the one you sewed inside, so one cover fits all.

`python3 build.py` writes `docs/notebooks/<slug>/<slug>-cover.pdf` (two covers
per sheet, same marks as the books).

## Collections

A collection is a set of notebooks given together, stacked and wrapped with a
paper band closed at the back with a wax seal. `python3 build.py` writes
`docs/collections/<slug>/<slug>-band.pdf`: four 9.75 × 1.5 in bands per letter
sheet (landscape, single-sided). The front of the band shows the series, the
collection name and the notebook numbers in their own colours; fold at the
small ticks, overlap the ends at the back, seal.
