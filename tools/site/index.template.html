How to make one

You will need

  • Text paper: US Letter, 24 lb (90 gsm). Cream looks like a real book.
  • Cover: 65 lb cardstock. Kraft looks the part; lighter colours show the red accent better.
  • A printer that prints both sides, or patience to flip sheets by hand.
  • A cutter: a rotary trimmer, or a sharp craft knife, a steel straightedge and a cutting mat.
  • A bone folder (or the back of a spoon) for crisp folds.
  • To bind: a sewing machine with a denim needle (100/16) and top-stitch thread; or a needle and thread by hand; or a long-reach stapler.
  • Optional: a 1/4 in (R6) corner rounder.

One print run (1 cover sheet + the interior sheets) makes two books. For 25 books, print 13 runs.

Before your first print

Print the alignment test double-sided and hold it up to a window. If the two crosshairs line up within a millimetre or two, you are ready. If not, the README explains how to build files corrected for your printer.

Print settings

  • Scale: 100% / Actual size. Never “fit to page.”
  • Two-sided, flip on long edge.
  • Cardstock: if your printer will not print both sides of it, print page 1, put the sheet back in, and print page 2.
  • Inkjet on cardstock: choose a matte paper type and high quality for richer colour.

  1. Cut the sheets in half

    Cut every sheet on the dashed line across the middle. Keep the top halves and the bottom halves in two piles: each pile is one complete book.

  2. Check the order

    A small grey label in the margin says “sheet 3 of 6.” Sheet 1 goes on the outside, the last sheet in the centre.

  3. Fold

    Fold each half sheet on its own, the side marked “front” outside, through the short fold marks at the top and bottom centre. Crease with the bone folder. Score the cover before folding it.

  4. Nest

    Slide the last sheet inside the one before, and so on out to sheet 1, then put everything inside the cover. Tap the spine and the top edge on the table to square them.

  5. Sew

    Open the book flat, cover side up, and sew straight down the fold with the longest straight stitch. Do not backstitch (it perforates the paper): leave tails, pull them inside and tie them at the centre spread. No machine? A three-hole pamphlet stitch by hand, or two staples, work too.

  6. Trim

    Cut on the light grey crosses at the corners of the cover, in any order: each line runs a little way along its edge, so it is still there after you trim the other edges. A rotary trimmer or a knife with a steel straightedge both work.

  7. Round the corners

    Round the two corners of the open side with a 1/4 in corner rounder. Done.

About

Tolle Lege collects short classics on attention, study and the spiritual life, always in the most recent translation that is in the public domain, set for a pocket and made by hand. It began as booklets for a student reading group at Calvin University.

The texts are in the public domain. The layouts and editorial pages are shared under CC BY 4.0 and the code under the MIT license. Everything, including the code that builds these files, is on GitHub.
